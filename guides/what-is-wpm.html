What Is WPM? Understanding Typing Speed

By Ayush Nandanwar · Updated November 18, 2026

Words per minute (WPM) is a common way to describe typing speed. It provides a convenient comparison, but the number depends on how a test measures words, time, and errors.

What does one “word” mean in a typing test?

Many typing tests use a standardized word length of five characters, including spaces and punctuation. This allows tests to compare passages with different actual word lengths. Under that convention, 250 characters typed in one minute equals 50 raw WPM.

Example: 250 typed characters ÷ 5 characters per standard word ÷ 1 minute = 50 raw WPM.

Raw WPM and net WPM

Raw WPM estimates typing pace without subtracting errors. Net WPM attempts to account for mistakes. Different tests use different error rules, so scores from two websites may not be directly comparable.

For example, a test might subtract an error penalty from raw speed. Another might count only correctly typed characters. Check the test's scoring explanation before comparing results.

Why accuracy matters

A fast typing pace is useful only if the text is correct enough for the task. Frequent corrections interrupt flow and can create additional work. Accuracy helps show whether a speed increase is controlled or simply rushed.

What affects your score?

  • Passage length and vocabulary
  • Punctuation, capitalization, and numbers
  • Keyboard layout and familiarity
  • Test duration
  • How errors and corrections are counted
  • Attention, fatigue, and interruptions

How to compare results fairly

Use the same test duration, similar passage difficulty, and the same keyboard when tracking progress. Take several tests and look at the average or trend. One score is not a complete picture of your typing ability.

Use WPM as feedback, not a goal by itself

For everyday writing, coding, study, or data entry, a comfortable pace with reliable accuracy may be more useful than maximizing a test score. Track speed alongside accuracy and how comfortable the session felt.
